Delivery / five stages

A clear line
from idea
to handover.

A project works when content decisions, site information and production responsibilities meet at the right moment. We make those handovers visible.

The sequence below describes our proposed way of working. No schedule, fee, supplier availability or venue suitability is confirmed through this website.
01

Frame the invitation

Agree who the project is for, what should be understood and which content has an owner.

The handover

Client: identify the content approver and venue contact. Gatherline: produce a concise project frame with open decisions.

02

Draw the visitor sequence

Test how arrival, focused content, participation and departure relate to each other.

The handover

Gatherline: develop schematic options and the content hierarchy. Client: approve the intended experience before detailed work.

03

Resolve the production package

Turn the approved direction into coordinated graphic, object, host and supplier briefs.

The handover

Appointed specialists and venue: confirm actual dimensions, technical design and installation conditions. Gatherline: coordinate the agreed information.

04

Prepare the room and hosts

Bring the content, supplier work and hosting sequence together against the agreed programme.

The handover

Suppliers: deliver their agreed scope. Client and venue: resolve operating responsibilities. Gatherline: coordinate its agreed installation and handover tasks.

05

Close and hand over

Agree what returns, what is retained and what should be recorded for the next use of the content.

The handover

Client and suppliers: own returns and asset disposition. Gatherline: compile the agreed content/host handover and close open coordination items.

The content owner

A named person approves facts, objects, wording and permissions. Any actual guest information, image/audio rights or consent process stays with the responsible organisation.

The venue owner

The actual operator supplies reliable site information and agrees how the venue can be used. Our concept diagrams are not measured drawings or operating approvals.

The specialist owner

Technical design, fabrication, installation and any relevant reviews belong to specifically appointed people. A studio illustration does not replace that work.

Before a design is fixed

The unknowns
need somewhere
to live.

We keep a visible list of open decisions: content readiness, installation conditions, weather response for outdoor spaces, ownership and the available programme. An unresolved item stays open until its responsible person resolves it.

For a courtyard project, an indoor fallback and weather decision point need agreement. For a mixed audience, content modes and hosting deserve an explicit discussion. Neither is automatically solved by a plan on a screen.
