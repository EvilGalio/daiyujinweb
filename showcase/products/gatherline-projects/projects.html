Design Week Room

01 / Materials exhibition
180 m² imagined programme / Indoor workshop
The audience
Design-aware visitors and local makers
The invitation
Make the work behind a finished object visible.

Five content stations. Original schematic, not to scale. No navigation, access or life-safety assurance.

  1. Arrival frame — Read the room question and choose where to begin.
  2. Material library — Compare three sample families by texture, finish and intended use.
  3. Assembly table — Follow a simple component sequence with a host explaining the choices.
  4. Maker conversation — Use a prompt card to ask about a material or making decision.
  5. Take-away shelf — Choose a printed material note to continue the story after leaving.

The maker collective would approve object information and handling permissions. The venue and appointed specialists would resolve actual dimensions, capacity, access, structure and operations.

The commission

An imagined local maker collective wants its materials and methods to be as memorable as its finished pieces. The room invites a visitor to compare, try and ask, without relying on a long introductory talk.

The spatial response

An orange arrival frame opens a view through the room. Low sample plinths leave the making table visible beyond them; the conversation area sits to one side so that a longer discussion can happen without becoming the only way through.

The production package

Content hierarchy, station layouts, sample labels, a table activity script and a host handover. A specialist fabricator would develop the temporary joinery and a venue team would confirm installation conditions.

Launch Courtyard

02 / Homeware pop-up
120 m² imagined programme / Courtyard concept
The audience
Invited trade visitors and interested neighbours
The invitation
Give a small collection enough room to be understood.

Five content stations. Original schematic, not to scale. No navigation, access or life-safety assurance.

  1. Arrival desk — Meet the host and understand the small collection being introduced.
  2. Product island — Look closely at form, glaze and the relationship between pieces.
  3. Demonstration bar — Watch a host show how the pieces fit into a table setting.
  4. Host table — Discuss a use case or question with the collection team.
  5. Departure note — Take a collection overview and choose an independent next step.

The brand would approve product information and samples. Weather, an indoor fallback, surface conditions, any overhead elements and the real operating plan would be resolved before an outdoor installation is agreed.

The commission

An invented independent homeware team is introducing a small ceramic collection. It needs a temporary setting where people can look closely, see how pieces are used and talk to a host before deciding what to explore next.

The spatial response

A low orange product island establishes the first focal point. A demonstration bar sits behind it, while a host table at the planted edge supports a longer conversation. The arrival and departure notes stay separate from the product display.

The production package

Product display hierarchy, a demonstration sequence, host prompts, arrival graphics and a supplier handover. Temporary furniture, lighting and any canopy would require a venue-specific specialist design.

Community Archive

03 / Neighbourhood exhibition
90 m² imagined programme / Small indoor gallery
The audience
A mixed local audience
The invitation
Let ordinary objects hold the centre of the story.

Five content stations. Original schematic, not to scale. No navigation, access or life-safety assurance.

  1. Room guide — Read the exhibition question and the available ways to explore.
  2. Object display — Look at an everyday object and its short, imagined context.
  3. Listening desk — Explore the outline of an audio story, with a text alternative planned.
  4. Shared-memory table — Use a fictional prompt to discuss what an ordinary object can carry.
  5. Reflection wall — Leave with a question to notice in the everyday world.

In a real project the content owner would clear object loans, image/audio rights and any consent process before use. All objects, voices and histories here are invented; the website collects no memories or guest information.

The commission

An invented cultural team wants to build a temporary exhibition around the ordinary things a neighbourhood keeps. The concept gives a cup, key and cloth their own context without claiming real provenance or recording personal histories online.

The spatial response

A compact guide introduces the room. Objects sit beside concise captions, a listening desk provides one content mode and a shared table creates a separate place for conversation. A reflection wall closes the visit without making participation compulsory.

The production package

Story structure, object/caption schedule, listening-content outline, table prompts and a host brief. Display cases, audio equipment and installation would be resolved with appropriate suppliers and the venue.
