01 / Scope & evidence

What the review can speak to.

The working question: where do responsibilities and evidence need to be clearer before the next handover?

First Review covers one legal entity and three named business systems: workplace identity, the order application and the shared document workspace. Five stakeholder conversations and supplied process notes form the evidence set.

  • Participants: the internal IT manager, an IT-provider representative, operations lead, procurement lead and a business process owner.
  • Sample materials: a joiner/mover/leaver checklist, an interruption contact sheet and a supplier register.
  • Effort: five consulting days arranged across two calendar weeks.

All materials and observations here are synthetic. No accounts, credentials, production records or supplier systems were accessed. No technical testing, recovery exercise or audit was performed.

02 / Three observations

Keep the evidence beside the action.

These are process observations for discussion. The sequencing windows describe a possible order of work; they are not a technical severity rating or a statement that risk will be removed.

R01 / Access handoverFirst 30 days

Name the final handover verifier.

What the sample evidence says
The synthetic joiner/mover/leaver checklist names an internal requestor and an IT-provider implementer, but no role verifies that the handover is complete.
The question to resolve
Who confirms that the requested change matches the completed change, and where is that confirmation recorded?
A bounded next action
Agree a final verifier role with the IT provider. Add a completion check to the handover record and try it on an anonymised example.
What remains unknown
No user account or live access record was inspected. The review does not establish whether any access is inappropriate.

Proposed owner: Internal IT manager, with the IT provider

R02 / Interruption readinessDays 31–60

Put alternate decisions in the plan.

What the sample evidence says
The sample interruption contact sheet names operational roles, but does not say who can take the decision when the primary role-holder is unavailable.
The question to resolve
If a primary decision owner cannot be reached, who has the authority and context to act?
A bounded next action
Agree alternate decision owners for the chosen interruption scenario. Use a short discussion exercise to check the contact and escalation sequence.
What remains unknown
No response exercise or recovery test took place. The document review cannot establish how people would act during a real interruption.

Proposed owner: Operations lead, supported by internal IT

R03 / Supplier contextDays 61–90

Connect suppliers to the work.

What the sample evidence says
The illustrative supplier register lists contract owners but does not identify the business process depending on each named supplier.
The question to resolve
Which day-to-day activity would need a different arrangement if this supplier was unavailable?
A bounded next action
Add the supported business process and its owner for up to five chosen suppliers. Record unanswered dependency questions for follow-up.
What remains unknown
No supplier was contacted or audited. The sample evidence does not establish a supplier control standard or assurance rating.

Proposed owner: Procurement lead, with business process owners

03 / Suggested sequencing

A manageable handover.

At the readout, confirm whether the proposed role can accept the action, what approval it needs and whether the example timing fits actual capacity.

First 30 days / R01

Access handover

Internal IT manager, with the IT provider.

Days 31–60 / R02

Interruption readiness

Operations lead, supported by internal IT.

Days 61–90 / R03

Supplier context

Procurement lead, with business process owners.

The sequence is illustrative, not a remedial deadline or a guarantee of security. The organisation owns the decision and implementation.

04 / Handover & limits

Leave the unanswered questions visible.

The readout turns the register into a conversation with the people who can act. It does not silently assign obligations to someone who was not involved.

  1. Confirm which observations are supported by the agreed evidence and record any disagreement.
  2. Ask each proposed owner whether they can accept the next action and what support they need.
  3. Record the decision, dependencies and next review point alongside the action.

Live access configuration, recovery capability, supplier controls and the completeness of the underlying records remain unverified. Those areas would need separately scoped work. This example is neither a certification nor assurance that the organisation is secure.
