01 / Where should we start?

First Review

A clear starting point for the work ahead.

Bring the people, process notes and system context together. We turn a defined evidence set into an action register your team can discuss and own.

Typical working shape
5 consulting days across 2 weeks
Starting scope
1 legal entity · up to 3 named systems · up to 5 stakeholder interviews

Bring to preparation

  • A high-level list of the three systems in scope.
  • Current access, handover and interruption process notes.
  • The people who own IT delivery and business decisions.

Leave with

  • A prioritised action register with evidence notes.
  • Named ownership questions and a practical readout.
  • An explicit record of what was not reviewed.
Boundary: No penetration testing, asset-wide scanning, certification or continuous monitoring.

02 / Who decides when work is interrupted?

Tabletop Day

Rehearse the decisions before the disruption.

Work through one agreed interruption scenario in a facilitated room. Pause at the decision points, clarify who is involved and record the follow-up work.

Typical working shape
1 workshop day, with preparation and readout across 2 weeks
Starting scope
1 fictional scenario · up to 8 participants

Bring to preparation

  • A current interruption or incident plan, if you have one.
  • A list of decision-making roles and alternate contacts.
  • An agreed operational scenario and workshop participants.

Leave with

  • An agreed scenario pack and decision log.
  • Observations on roles, communications and handovers.
  • An action note with proposed follow-up owners.
Boundary: No live attack, production recovery exercise, system changes or active incident response.

03 / What do we depend on?

Supplier Map

See the business behind the supplier list.

Connect a small set of business-critical suppliers to the processes they support. Separate what is known from the questions that still need an owner.

Typical working shape
3 consulting days across 2 weeks
Starting scope
1 legal entity · up to 5 named business-critical suppliers

Bring to preparation

  • A list of the five suppliers to discuss and their contract owners.
  • The business processes that rely on each supplier.
  • Existing supplier documents or answers, without confidential details.

Leave with

  • A readable business-dependency map.
  • An evidence-request list with unanswered questions.
  • A supplier follow-up register with owner roles.
Boundary: No supplier audit, assurance rating or verification of every supplier assertion.

Before making a decision

Know where the edges are.

How is the fee agreed?

We agree a written fixed-fee scope after the preparation conversation. The three service descriptions give a starting size and deliverable set; they are not instant quotes. Additional entities, systems, participants or supplier groups need an explicit scope decision.

Will you need access to our systems?

These advisory starting points use agreed conversations and document review. The website does not request credentials, system addresses, live records or uploads. Any evidence-handling arrangement belongs in the written scope before work begins.

Does a First Review certify that we are secure?

No. It describes the agreed evidence, questions and proposed actions inside a defined review boundary. It does not certify compliance, confirm the absence of vulnerabilities or guarantee prevention of a breach.

Can you help during an active disruption?

These services are planned advisory work. Stonepath does not operate an emergency incident-response line or a security operations centre. Follow your organisation's existing incident arrangements and designated response provider for an active situation.
