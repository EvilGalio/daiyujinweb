We are a six-person team of interior designers and project coordinators. Our work begins with the quiet, practical questions that make a room feel like it belongs to you.

Living with the result

Less starting over.
More looking closely.

When the studio began in 2018, we chose to work at the scale of everyday life: a kitchen, a living room, a small shopfront. That is still the scale we care about.

A first conversation might spend as much time on the crowded counter or the chair by the window as on new materials. Understanding those things makes a smaller, clearer brief possible.

One studio contact coordinates the agreed work, with design and documentation support from the wider team. You know which decision comes next and who is responsible for it.

Inside the reading room
How we work together

Clear decisions.
Room for your point of view.

We start with what you have.

Existing furniture, daylight, routines and constraints belong in the brief. Keeping an object because it is useful or loved is a design decision in its own right.

We make the scope readable.

Deliverables, design stages and exclusions are written down before work begins. A material direction is different from a technical specification, and a design fee is separate from a supply or building allowance.

We explain the choices.

Layouts and material proposals come with reasons. You can see how a change responds to the room, rather than being asked to choose from an unrelated collection of finishes.

We name the handover.

We provide the agreed design intent. The owner appoints the required contractors and specialists, who verify technical conditions, approvals and installation before work proceeds.

Small hospitality,
with a daily rhythm.

The same attention works at a neighbourhood counter. We consider the front-of-house experience, the things a team reaches for and how visitors are intended to move through the room.

Operational, food-premises and approval requirements belong with the operator and appropriately appointed specialists.
