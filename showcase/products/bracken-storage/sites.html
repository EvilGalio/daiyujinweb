01 / Load shifting

An uneven working day.

An independent component-repair workshop. Daytime machining and occasional late finishing make the operating pattern more useful than the floor area alone.

Working pattern
Daytime work + late finishing
Start with
Interval usage and machine schedules
Prepare a similar site review

The question

Could a defined storage operating pattern fit the workshop’s uneven day?

The evidence to gather

Review interval usage alongside job schedules, rather than assuming every weekday is alike. Identify which upcoming equipment or working-hour changes would alter the assessment.

The people to involve

The workshop manager provides the operating record and nominates a site decision-maker. Bracken coordinates the assessment scope; the specialist design team reviews the electrical and equipment interfaces.

This brief does not assign battery capacity or claim a bill reduction. A photographed cabinet is not a specified system.

02 / Solar self-use

A roof with another role.

A produce packhouse with an existing solar roof. Seasonal refrigeration and packing work mean a busy harvest week tells only part of the energy story.

Working pattern
Seasonal packing + refrigeration
Start with
Solar generation and seasonal demand
Prepare a similar site review

The question

How do the timing of solar generation and the packhouse’s demand compare?

The evidence to gather

Bring generation and usage records covering both busy and quieter periods. Include planned changes to refrigeration or handling equipment and the current solar system information.

The people to involve

The facilities team gathers records and appoints the people responsible for the solar and electrical systems. Bracken coordinates those interfaces before an equipment option is considered.

No self-use percentage, export value or payback is assumed. The assessment must account for the site’s actual seasonal pattern.

03 / Resilience assessment

Keep the priorities clear.

A shared workspace with daytime tenants, meeting rooms and essential communications. The first task is agreeing which activities need priority during an interruption.

Working pattern
Daytime tenants + meetings
Start with
Essential loads and continuity priorities
Prepare a similar site review

The question

Which clearly named activities should a resilience assessment examine?

The evidence to gather

Separate communications and other agreed priorities from the full building demand. Record existing continuity arrangements, who makes decisions and what each responsible party needs to review.

The people to involve

The building operator agrees priorities with tenants. Bracken organises a bounded specialist assessment; the detailed design establishes whether a proposed backup function is feasible.

No uninterrupted service or backup duration is promised. Ordinary battery storage should not be assumed to provide a backup function.
