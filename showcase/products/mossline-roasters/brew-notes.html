A little help at the kitchen bench

Make a cup.
Make it yours.

Three starting recipes, with a little room for your own taste. A scale helps you repeat what works. Your brewer instructions always come first.

Paper filter · a cup to linger over

The patient pour.

15g coffee / 250g water

  1. Set a filter paper in your brewer, rinse with hot water and discard the rinse water. Add 15g of filter-ground coffee.
  2. Pour just enough water to wet the grounds. Pause for about 30 seconds.
  3. Pour the remaining water gently in stages, keeping below the top of the paper. Let it drain before lifting the brewer.
  4. Taste once it has cooled a little. Keep the dose steady and make one small grind adjustment next time.

Espresso · short and familiar

A small daily ritual.

18g in / 36g out

  1. Use a basket intended for an 18g dose. Begin with finely ground coffee and a clean, dry basket.
  2. Distribute the coffee evenly, tamp level and lock the basket into the machine.
  3. Watch the drink weight and stop near 36g. Use your machine instructions for operation and cleaning.
  4. Keep a note of the dose, yield and taste. Adjust your grinder if needed; a fixed pre-ground option is only a starting point.

Plunger · enough to share

Put the kettle on.

30g coffee / 500g water

  1. Choose a plunger large enough for 500g of water. Add 30g of coarse-ground coffee.
  2. Add hot water carefully, leave headroom and give the grounds a gentle stir.
  3. Let it steep for about four minutes. Fit the lid and press gently; never force a stuck plunger.
  4. Pour into cups rather than leaving the brewed coffee sitting on the grounds. Adjust the coffee dose next time if you prefer it stronger or lighter.

These are starting recipes, not fixed performance claims. The paper-filter method is informed by Hario's basic guide; equipment capacity and instructions may differ. We do not sell or endorse a specific brewer here.

Before the next cup

Keep the good
bits simple.

Keep your coffee closed in a cool, dry cupboard, away from bright light and strong smells. If you have a grinder, weigh and grind the amount you need for the next brew.

Clean your brewer according to its instructions. A familiar recipe and a clean setup give you a useful way to compare one coffee with another.
