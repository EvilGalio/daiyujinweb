A role needs to make sense in the context of your life and the work you want to do. Start with the responsibilities, the working week and what you would want to understand about the team.

A useful first read

Read beyond
the title.

Look for the work you would own, who you would report to and how the team is organised. Then check the location, working pattern and what is included in the stated pay.

Our three fictional role records include those details. They also distinguish the evidence that matters from experience that would simply be helpful.

Before a conversation

Prepare a relevant
piece of your story.

  1. Choose a responsibility.Pick one part of the role and a past situation that helps explain how you approach that work.
  2. Explain the choices you made.Describe the context, the action you took and what you learned. Use your own account; keep employer details confidential.
  3. Bring the questions still open.Ask about priorities, work handovers, manager support and how a decision would be communicated.

A role works both ways

What would help
you decide?

The actual working week. Confirm the location, hours, breaks and any changeable arrangements in a real process.

The team around you. Understand the reporting line, daily support and how competing priorities are handled.

The full terms. Benefits, the employing entity and complete terms would need to be disclosed separately; an illustrative pay range is not an offer.

The next conversation. Know its purpose, who will be involved and which questions remain unanswered.

A respectful boundary.

There is no need to share a CV, personal information, salary history or confidential work here. This website has no application, upload or candidate ranking.

Our guides are free local downloads. No one receives your reading choices or contacts you after a visit.

Are these roles available to apply for?

No. All three roles and their employers are fictional examples. Each complete record demonstrates the information a role could provide; there is no vacancy or application.

Can I send a CV?

No. This concept does not accept CVs, personal details or messages. Keep your information with you.

Do the pay ranges describe the market?

No. The annual base ranges are invented for the three example roles. They are not market pay guidance or an employment offer.

Does reading a role assess my suitability?

No. The site has no scoring, screening or candidate assessment. The examples and guide help frame questions for a conversation.
