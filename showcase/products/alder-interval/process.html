Working together

A clear next step.
At every stage.

A commission does not need to begin with every answer. It does need a shared question, an agreed scope and a way to decide what happens next.

01

Begin with the brief

Agree who the project serves, what should change and which decisions the first stage must support. Separate known information from assumptions.

At the end: An agreed question, information list and first-stage scope.

02

Read the place

Review the available records and discuss the existing building or site. Identify gaps before treating a drawing or photograph as evidence.

At the end: A record of constraints, valued features and unresolved questions.

03

Draw the alternatives

Explore a small set of spatial options. Compare how each supports the brief, what it retains and where further advice is needed.

At the end: An options discussion and a recorded design direction.

04

Coordinate the direction

Develop the selected design with the appointed consultants and the client. Keep drawings, changes and ownership of decisions together.

At the end: An agreed design package and a clear list of next responsibilities.

Before the first conversation

Bring what you know.
Leave space for what you do not.

A short description of the intended use, an indicative area if known, the project location and your current stage are enough to prepare an initial conversation. Existing drawings are useful, but their absence is a question to discuss.

Our online preparation tool uses only those broad choices. It does not ask you to upload drawings, reveal an address or send personal information.

Fees, programme and the wider team depend on the actual brief. There is no automatic estimate or promised start date in this demonstration.

Your decisions.

You set the intended use, priorities, decision-makers and available resources. At each stage, we record what you have chosen and what remains open.

The wider team.

Surveys, technical design, cost advice, statutory processes and construction are scoped with the appropriate parties. An architectural sketch is not permission to build.
