01

Routine examination

Get to know where things stand.

A conversation about your dental care, followed by an examination of your teeth, gums and mouth. There is room to ask questions and understand what may come next.

What the conversation covers

  • A discussion before the examination
  • An explanation of the findings
  • A conversation about next steps and future reviews

An examination is not a promise that treatment will be completed at the same visit. Any further assessment or treatment would be discussed separately.

02

Hygiene visit

Everyday care, talked through.

A visit focused on oral hygiene, with assessment, cleaning as appropriate and a discussion of everyday care. What happens is based on an individual assessment.

What the conversation covers

  • Time to discuss your usual oral-care routine
  • A hygiene assessment and cleaning as appropriate
  • Practical questions about everyday care

A hygiene visit does not replace an examination or guarantee a particular result. The appropriate approach would be agreed with the actual clinical team.

Ask about this visit↗
03

Restorative consultation

Understand the options first.

A conversation about a dental repair question. An assessment helps establish which options might be relevant and whether further information is needed.

What the conversation covers

  • Time to explain the question in a real consultation
  • Discussion of possible options and limitations
  • A written estimate before any agreed treatment

A consultation is not a treatment booking. No specific restoration, price or outcome can be guaranteed from this website.

Ask about this visit↗

Know the plan
before saying yes.

In a real practice, findings, options and costs would be explained before agreed treatment. A first conversation does not commit you to treatment.

Follow-up intervals are individual. The NHS explains what a dental check-up involves; this site does not set a universal recall schedule.

Need actual urgent dental help?

This fictional practice cannot provide care or assess symptoms. Contact an actual dentist or read NHS urgent dental guidance.
