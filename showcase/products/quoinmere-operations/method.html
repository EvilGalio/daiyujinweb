Method / From first question to working agreement

Keep the scope small.
Make the handover clear.

We start with one operating situation, work with the people who receive the output, and leave the next review in their hands.

  1. Frame one question.

    Agree the work area, the decision owner and the working document you need. Name exclusions before the engagement begins.

  2. Look at the present work.

    Use an agreed observation, a redacted example or a walkthrough. Separate what has been seen from what is still an assumption.

  3. Make a working version.

    Draft a record, agenda or tracker with the people who will use it. Rehearse the awkward handover, not just the straightforward one.

  4. Try a bounded change.

    Choose a small setting and a review point with the owner. The team decides whether the working version is suitable for its operation.

  5. Hand over ownership.

    Leave the document, unresolved questions and the next review date. Further support is separately scoped; it is not an automatic retainer.

What needs agreement

Access, roles and boundaries
are part of the work.

The business owns decisions.

We facilitate and organise. Operational approval, staffing decisions and acceptance remain with the responsible people in the business.

People need a useful invitation.

Explain the question, what preparation is needed and who will act on the output. A large group may need smaller preparatory conversations.

Documents need care.

Agree appropriate handling before sharing operational material in a real engagement. This website accepts no files, employee data or confidential examples.

Fees, access, responsibilities, exclusions and the actual timetable would be agreed in writing. Example sequences are not availability, price or outcome commitments.
