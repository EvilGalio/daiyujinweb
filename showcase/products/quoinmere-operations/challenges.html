QO / 01 / EXAMPLE SEQUENCE: 2 WEEKS

Shift Handover

The shift changes. The open question stays.

A maker or distributor whose work passes between shifts, desks or teams without a dependable record.

What you may recognise

  • The same question gets asked at the start of each shift.
  • Open items travel in personal messages.
  • The person taking over cannot see who owns the next action.

What the work produces

  • A one-page handover record with an owner for each open item.
  • A short observation log to test what people actually use.
  • A review rule for closing, carrying or escalating an item.

A bounded sequence

  1. Observe one handover and describe the current record.
  2. Draft a small record with the people receiving the work.
  3. Try it in an agreed setting and hand ownership to the team.

The starting question: What must the next shift know, and who owns what is still open?

The 2-week sequence is illustrative. Access, people and an agreed scope determine a real timetable. This is not a savings forecast, an operational diagnosis or a booking.

QO / 02 / EXAMPLE SEQUENCE: 3 WEEKS

Planning Cadence

A meeting should leave a decision behind.

An operating team with recurring meetings, shifting priorities and unresolved exceptions between planning cycles.

What you may recognise

  • The weekly meeting repeats yesterday’s status.
  • An exception appears in several different lists.
  • No one knows which meeting can make the next decision.

What the work produces

  • A decision calendar with a reason for each meeting.
  • An exception list with a named decision owner.
  • A simple preparation and follow-through rule.

A bounded sequence

  1. Map current meetings and the decisions they are meant to support.
  2. Separate information updates from decisions and exceptions.
  3. Rehearse a shorter agenda and transfer ownership of the cadence.

The starting question: Which decisions belong in which meeting, and what happens between them?

The 3-week sequence is illustrative. Access, people and an agreed scope determine a real timetable. This is not a savings forecast, an operational diagnosis or a booking.

QO / 03 / EXAMPLE SEQUENCE: 4 WEEKS

New-site Readiness

Opening day needs an owner for every handover.

A regional maker or distributor preparing one new location, with operational workstreams that need to come together.

What you may recognise

  • Workstream checklists do not share a common owner view.
  • A completed task is mistaken for an accepted handover.
  • An opening date hides dependencies that remain unresolved.

What the work produces

  • A readiness tracker separating complete, accepted and open work.
  • A rehearsal agenda for the operating team.
  • An open-item handover with decision owners.

A bounded sequence

  1. Map operating handovers and name the receiving roles.
  2. Make dependencies and acceptance questions visible.
  3. Rehearse the handover and leave a clear open-item record.

The starting question: What is ready to hand over, what is still open, and who can decide?

The 4-week sequence is illustrative. Access, people and an agreed scope determine a real timetable. This is not a savings forecast, an operational diagnosis or a booking.
