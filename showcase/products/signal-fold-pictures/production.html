The practical plan and the creative idea belong in the same conversation. Our proposed process keeps the audience, scope and final use visible from the start.

Find the intention

Agree the audience, the viewing context and the one thing a viewer should carry away. A short treatment turns that intention into a story that can be discussed.

Output: a written treatment, reference direction and a list of open scope questions.

Plan the shoot

Identify the people, locations, product samples, access and setups needed. Landscape and vertical coverage are planned before the camera arrives.

Output: a proposed shot list, schedule, responsibility map and permissions review. No date or crew is booked by this website.

Build the edit

Start with structure and meaning. Then refine picture rhythm, sound direction and graphics around the approved story. Review owners and rounds are agreed in the project scope.

Output: an agreed review sequence and a visible change list. New scenes, formats or reshoots are separately scoped.

Finish the handover

Check the approved edit list, aspect ratios, captions, sound and any agreed title versions. Name the files so the receiving team can identify the right use.

Output: an agreed export matrix and delivery checklist. Usage permissions remain a separate documented requirement.

The export list matters

One story.
More than one frame.

A hero edit and two cutdowns in two aspect ratios make six proposed exports. That does not mean six separate films, or that an automatic crop is enough.

The production brief makes that matrix visible so framing and edit requirements can be discussed together.

Do you provide a fixed price here?

No. Access, shoot scope, contributors, rights, edit requirements and usage all affect a separately agreed production scope. This website does not issue a price or binding offer.

Can I upload footage for an edit?

No. The brief can record that footage already exists, then add source, rights and technical review questions. No file transfer or editing service is connected.

Are the concept stills actual film frames?

No. They are original generated stills created for these fictional treatments. There is no showreel, released film, soundtrack or client commission behind them.

Who owns the permissions question?

A real project would identify an owner for participant, location, artwork, product and sound permissions before production. This demonstration cannot grant or verify rights.
