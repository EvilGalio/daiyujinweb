An initial conversation needs a clear description, not a shipment or confidential file. Use the checklist to prepare what a research team would need to discuss.

The information
around the item.

  1. PurposeThe research question and the decision the work should support.
  2. Material descriptionA non-confidential category, what is known about it and what remains unknown.
  3. Method contextThe current written-method version, readout and calculation convention, if these exist.
  4. Handover needsThe coded item count, report structure, exceptions and questions you want to discuss.

Before anything moves.

Material acceptance, appropriate handling, shipping and any physical work would require a separate project review and written instructions. This site provides none of those instructions and does not accept shipments.

Keep samples and files with your team. Do not include personal identifiers, clinical records, confidential sequences or proprietary protocols in any demonstration.

Keep the source identifiable.

A real agreed project would use a coded item manifest and a shared method version. A raw-value file, calculation note and exception register should describe the same project and records.

The synthetic report shows that relationship using invented record IDs. It does not read, analyse or store your data.

Common first questions

Can I send a sample through this website?

No. There is no sample ordering, shipment, upload or live lab service. The site demonstrates how an initial scope can be organised.

Does the report establish assay performance?

No. Its values are invented to show a reporting structure and explicit arithmetic. They establish no biological or clinical result.

What if my method is not written down yet?

Record that gap in the scope outline. A transfer or repeated-batch request needs the missing documentation discussed before work could be agreed.
