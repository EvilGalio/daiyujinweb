Import operations depend on handovers: an owner leaving context that another person can use. That is the part of the day we chose to build for.

Rotterdam roots.
A focused product.

Tallyharbor began in 2021 with a simple product question: how could a small import team keep the next order action beside the record that explains it?

Today our 22-person, bootstrapped team brings product design, engineering and onboarding together around that question. We work with European importing businesses whose order follow-up is still spread across sheets and messages.

How we make decisions

Keep the next action
honest and understandable.

Context before activity.

A button click matters when the next person can understand its effect. We keep the original date, document identity and order ownership visible.

Make scope explicit.

Order follow-up is our focus. We do not quietly turn document presence into compliance approval or a revised date into live tracking.

Map the real handover.

Onboarding starts with the fields and responsibilities your team already uses. We agree how they fit before treating a plan as the answer.

The people behind the workspace

Product decisions
meet operational questions.

Our product and engineering team maintains the connected order record and its everyday tools. Onboarding colleagues help teams translate their current order fields and document habits into a workable starting point.

We keep those conversations close. A confusing handover is useful product feedback, whether it begins with a missing document, a date change or an unclear owner.
