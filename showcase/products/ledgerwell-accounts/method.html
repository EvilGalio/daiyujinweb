Our method

Less chasing.
A better handover.

The relationship works when the starting point, open questions and next responsibility are visible to everyone involved.

Start with what exists.

You do not need to pretend the records are tidier than they are. We begin with an inventory: available periods, file types, missing items and any work already completed.

A realistic starting point separates regular preparation from catch-up work. It also keeps an existing adviser’s requirements in view.

One shared working rhythm

Four steps, with a named owner.

Agree the starting point

Owner and coordinator list the periods, record sources and intended output. Scope, fees and timing are agreed separately before any engagement.

Organise the handover

The owner supplies the agreed evidence through a separately arranged secure route. The bookkeeping group records what has arrived and what is absent.

Resolve the open items

One consolidated questions list goes back to the owner. Unanswered points remain visible in the working files; we do not silently treat them as resolved.

Return a usable pack

Prepared files and an outstanding-items schedule go to the agreed recipient. A management discussion or adviser handover follows the service selected.

A few sensible
questions first.

Practical details matter more than a promise to make everything disappear.

Can you work with incomplete records?

A gap inventory can be a useful starting point. What can be prepared, and when, depends on what is missing. The enquiry checklist records those gaps rather than promising completion.

Does this replace my statutory adviser?

No. That adviser retains their separately agreed responsibilities and any filing. Year-end Ready prepares an indexed handover for their review.

Can I send my records through this website?

No. This fictional demonstration has no uploads, bank feed, mailbox or customer account. Use fictional choices only in the service-fit checklist.
